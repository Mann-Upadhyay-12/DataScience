Texas Grid Battery Dispatch & Valuation Lab

Empirical Day-Ahead Arbitrage, Walk-Forward ML Forecasting, and Linear Program Co-Optimization

The Question This Project Answers

Suppose you own a 100 MW / 200 MWh battery in Texas. Every morning, by 10:00, you must tell the grid operator (ERCOT) exactly when you will charge and discharge tomorrow. You get paid tomorrow's day-ahead price for every MWh you sell and pay it for every MWh you buy. How much money can you actually make — and how much of it depends on how well you forecast tomorrow's prices?

How a battery earns money, in 3 lines
  • Buy low, sell high: charge in cheap hours (night, sunny midday), discharge in expensive hours (evening peak).
  • Every round trip leaks energy: at 85% round-trip efficiency, 1 MWh bought returns only 0.85 MWh. So the sell price must beat the buy price by at least ~18% to be worth it.
  • You commit before you know: the schedule is locked using a forecast. If the forecast gets the cheap/expensive hours in the wrong order, money is left on the table. That missed money is called regret.

The pipeline, step by step

1 · CollectHourly ERCOT prices, load / wind / solar forecasts and weather, 2024 → Nov 2026. Each forecast is kept only if it was published before the 09:45 cutoff.
2 · ForecastPredict tomorrow's 24 hourly prices with simple rules (yesterday, last week, 7-day average) and ML models (Lasso, gradient boosting).
3 · OptimizeA linear program picks the best charge/discharge schedule for the forecast, respecting power, energy, efficiency and ≤1 cycle/day.
4 · SettleThat locked schedule is paid at the actual prices of the day. No peeking — this is a walk-forward backtest.
5 · ScoreCompare with the Oracle (perfect foresight). The ratio is the capture rate.

Read This Before Trusting the Numbers

Modelling simplifications
  • Price-taker: the battery's own trades are assumed not to move prices.
  • Hub prices, day-ahead only: no nodal congestion, no real-time market, no ancillary services (which are a large share of real Texas battery revenue).
  • Each day stands alone: the battery starts and ends every day empty, and is capped at 1 full cycle per day.
  • ML models were trained for HB_NORTH only. Other hubs are compared using the simple-rule strategies.
Why the same hub shows two different revenues The ML models need ~6 months of history before their first forecast, so the head-to-head comparison including ML uses 855 days (from 2024-07-01). The siting comparison uses all 1,030 days, which add the unusually lucrative first half of 2024. That's why HB_NORTH's Oracle is $36,463/MW-yr on tab 2 but $40,968/MW-yr on tab 5. Comparisons are only valid within one table.

Mandatory Cutoff Timing Audit

Day-ahead bids in Texas close at 10:00 Central. The pipeline enforces a cutoff at 09:45 Central on D-1: for every target day it uses only the latest load, wind and solar forecast vintage published before that time. Automated build tests found 0 forecasts published after the cutoff, 0 unlisted feature columns, 0 same-day actuals among the features, and 5 / 5 lag spot-checks passed across the 24,912 hourly feature rows.
